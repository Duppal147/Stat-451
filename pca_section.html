The 30 features are severely redundant: radius, perimeter and area are highly correlated(Fig 1). On standardised training data the first principal component carries 44.5% of the variance with all 30 features positive contribution, led by concavity and size; ten components carry 95% variance.

The ten span only 0.220–0.261, so PC1 is close to an unweighted average of the size and shape measurements rather than any one of them.

That one direction dominates motivated tuning the SVM over the number of components (Table 2). At its best C, a single component already reaches 0.912 recall and two reach 0.959; the grid's best cell, 15 components at C = 1, gains only 0.018 over two. PCA therefore tells us the predictors are essentially one-dimensional— but that component is harder to read than the raw measurements and buys little accuracy, so we model the original features.
